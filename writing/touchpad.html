what a touchpad actually measures

---------------------------------
2026-08-10

A swim touchpad is not a stopwatch. It is a large pressure switch wired into a console that is already counting, and the number on the scoreboard is just the moment that switch closed. Everything people argue about at the end of a race comes from the gap between the swimmer arriving and the switch closing.

The pad needs a real push, not a graze. A hand that slides across the very top of the pad can register late or not at all, which is why the console watches for a backup: a button pressed by a human timer behind the lane. When the pad and the button disagree by more than a set threshold, the operator decides which one is telling the truth, and the answer is usually the one that matches the video and the other lanes.

What I like about the job is how little of it is guessing. There is a start interface, a cable run, a console, and a pad, and when a time looks wrong the fault is almost always somewhere you can put your hand on. Wet connector, kinked cable, pad hung too high in the gutter. The system is honest about its own failures, which is more than most software manages.
