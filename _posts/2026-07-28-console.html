--- layout: post navKey: writing title: "the meet that ran on a backup button" description: "A dead touchpad, eleven heats, and one volunteer with a plunger." ---

Lane four went dead in the middle of a prelim session. Not a bad time, no time at all, the console just stopped seeing the pad. We had eleven heats left before the break and a deck full of parents who had driven two hours to watch.

This is what the backup exists for. A person stands behind the lane with a plunger in their hand and presses when the swimmer touches, and the console records that press in the same event as the pad. Officially it is the secondary system. For eleven heats it was the only system, and every one of those times went up because a volunteer pressed a button consistently for twenty minutes.

The fault turned out to be a connector that had been swimming in gutter water all morning. Ten seconds to fix once we found it, which is how these things usually go. What stuck with me was the design decision underneath: the people who built this equipment assumed the electronics would fail and put a human in the loop on purpose. Most systems I have worked on assume the opposite and have nothing behind them at all.
