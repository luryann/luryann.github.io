--- layout: post navKey: writing title: "the meet that ran on a backup button" description: "A dead touchpad, three hours, and two volunteers with a button." ---

Lane 11 just died in the middle of the morning session. Not that it was a bad time or anything, but the console suddenly stopped registering the pad. We had half the session left before the break and a deck full of parents who had gotten up at 5am to take their kids to the meet.

This is where backup buttons come in handy. Typically, two people stand at the blocks with a button in their hand and press it when the swimmer hits the touchpad, and the console records that button press as their time. It's officially the secondary system. For half the session though it was the only system, and every single one of those times went official because two volunteers consistently pressed a button for three hours straight.

The issue turned out to be a connector that had been swimming in gutter water all morning. It took like ten seconds to fix once we found it, which is how most these things go. What I found interesting though was the design for redundancy. The people who made this equipment assumed the electronics would fail eventually and put in a human as a backup.
