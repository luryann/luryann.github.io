--- layout: post navKey: writing title: "what a touchpad actually measures" description: "What a swim touchpad measures, and why the backup button exists." ---

A swim touchpad is a large pressure sensitive plate (piezoelectric switch) mounted on the finishing walls of each lane. It does not keep time internally. The timing system up on the deck begins timing on the start signal, then when a swimmer hits the touchpad hard enough to close the circuit, the console simply registers the time of contact and sends the information to the timing system. The rankings for every heat are made based on time between when the swimmer reaches the end wall and when the touchpad itself registers a hit.

For the time to count, you have to smack the thing. A light tap on the top edge may not complete the circuit at all. This is sometimes an issue with younger swimmers, who are inherently lighter and weaker, which means they sometimes lack the effort to push the pad hard enough for it to register after an exhausting event. The console is also monitoring a secondary input. Usually two timers at the end of the pool are pushing a separate button (called the "pickle") to mark the touch. If the two times differ by more than a set amount, it will get flagged for review. If the difference is too large, the time gets discarded.

One of the things I've always enjoyed about the job is the physical nature of the troubleshooting. When a race or relay has a mishap due to faulty systems, about 90% of the time you can simply see what's wrong on the timing system, walk to the lane, and look at the connections going into the wall plate or harness. The equipment practically talks to you when something is wrong, which I think is pretty awesome.
