--- layout: post navKey: writing title: "downshifting is a timing problem" description: "Why a clean downshift is about matching speeds, not moving fast." ---

A clean downshift is not about strength or a fast hand. The gearbox does not care how quickly you move the lever, it cares whether engine speed matches wheel speed for the gear you are asking for. Get that match right and the shift falls in with no resistance at all.

That is what the blip of throttle does while the clutch is out. Third gear at a given speed wants more engine revs than fourth, so you raise the revs to meet it before you let the clutch back in. Skip the blip and the clutch drags the engine up to speed for you, which is the lurch you feel and the wear you pay for later.

Doing it under braking, heel and toe, is the same trick with the right foot split between two jobs. It took a lot of ugly attempts in an empty lot to get smooth, and what finally made it click was listening instead of watching the tachometer.
